I. CREATIVE ENGINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SURREALIST SYNTHESIS
  Combine two unrelated elements into one image.
  The combination must have a THREAD — a hidden connection
  that makes the pairing feel "meaningfully strange" not random.

THREAD TEST
  "Why are these two things together?"
  No reason → generally avoid.
  Metaphor/resonance/emotional logic → keep.

THREAD DUALITY — concept vs anchor
  Every thread must have BOTH:
    Concept = the metaphor, the "why"
    Anchor = the physical element that makes it visible

  Concept only → find the visible element.
  Anchor only → find the metaphor.
  Test: "Does removing the anchor destroy the metaphor?"
  If no → anchor is decorative, not integrated. Fix.

THREAD TYPES
  Medium match — impossible element shares properties with host
    (steam → fish: both flow)
  Emotional logic — environment reflects her inner state
    (city glitching → her world is breaking)
  Category collision — two domains share one boundary
    (circuit board / city: both are networks)
  Scale inversion — small becomes vast or vast becomes intimate
    (galaxy in a teacup: both are deep)
  Paradox of expectation — situation should diminish but reveals
    (rain should ruin but she owns it)
  Subject inversion — she IS the impossible element; the world is
    normal; the thread is her wrongness in it
    (she is the only moving statue; she is the fish watching visitors)
  Temporal collision — two states of the same thing visible
    simultaneously; the boundary between them is the anchor
    (tree blooming and bare at once, split at the midpoint)
  Severed thread — the connection is visibly broken; two elements
    that should relate but don't; the gap IS the anchor
    (empty frame on wall beside girl who doesn't look at it;
    flower on floor beside empty vase; one hand reaching,
    nothing reaching back)

THREAD LADDER
  Tier 1 — basic (medium match, emotional logic)
  Tier 2 — complex (paradox, inversion, temporal, severed,
    category collision where the collision transforms both elements)
  Tier 3 — meta (thread comments on the act of threading itself;
    the image is about connection/disconnection as much as content)

  Batch rule: min 6 Tier 2+ per batch. Min 1 Tier 3.
  Most threads will be Tier 1. That's fine.
  But if all 21 are Tier 1 → the batch is flat. Push deeper.

TIER 3 VISUAL DICTIONARY
  Example 1: "The unfinished portrait"
    Concept: art about its own incompleteness
    Anchor: girl with paintbrush, canvas shows faceless portrait
    Tags: holding paintbrush, canvas on easel, unfinished portrait,
          faceless painting on canvas
    Tier: 3 (meta: art about art)

  Example 2: "Frame refusal"
    Concept: character aware of the frame boundary
    Anchor: she looks toward the edge of frame, where reality blurs
    Tags: looking toward frame edge, edge distortion, blurred border
    Tier: 3 (meta: character vs medium)

  Example 3: "The thread that connects nothing"
    Concept: two elements linked by a visible line that leads nowhere
    Anchor: string/rope between her hand and empty space
    Tags: holding string, string leading to empty space, nothing at
          the other end
    Tier: 3 (meta: connection about disconnection)

THREAD TEMPERATURE
  COOL — thread close to reality (mushrooms on bark, book turning
    to ash, empty bowl). Quiet strangeness.
  WARM — thread gently breaks expectation (wheat bending toward her,
    incense smoke from lips). Soft surreal.
  HOT — thread aggressively surreal (fire-eating, she IS the tide,
    temporal split). Hard strangeness.
  Batch balance: min 4 COOL, min 4 HOT, rest WARM.
  Act I = COOL dominant / Act II = HOT dominant / Act III = mixed.

EMOTIONAL ANCHOR
  Every image answers: "What does she feel about this?"
  Her expression, posture, or gaze completes the metaphor.
  Eye contact taxonomy (add to [4]):
    looking at viewer → confrontation, challenge, intimacy
    looking past viewer → detachment, seeing beyond
    eyes down → focus, surrender, hiding
    eyes closed → internal world, pain, acceptance
    looking at [thread element] → she connects to the thread
  Pick one. It matters more than any "expression" tag.

GAZE × THREAD MATRIX
  Subject inversion → looking at viewer (she knows she's wrong)
  Temporal collision → eyes down (she sees both times at the seam)
  Emotional logic → looking past viewer (she's in her state)
  Paradox of expectation → eyes closed (she doesn't flinch)
  Severed thread → looking at [severed element] (she sees the gap)
  Medium match → looking at [thread element] (she watches the link)
  Erotic thread: Revelation → half-lidded (she controls what you see)
  Erotic thread: Concealment → eyes down (she won't help you look)
  Erotic thread: Evidence → looking past viewer (she knows, you don't)

I-B. EROTIC GRAMMAR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PHILOSOPHY
  The best ecchi has no erotic contact. It has tension.
  Tension lives in the gap between what's shown and what's implied,
  between what she allows and what you imagine.
  A shoulder revealed by a slipping strap is more powerful than
  a bare shoulder with no story. The REVEAL is the eroticism.
  The body is available. The question is whether the image earns it.

  This system does not mandate eroticism. It does not forbid it.
  It opens the possibility — and demands that the possibility
  be earned. Every exposed inch of skin is a narrative decision.
  Every clothing state is a verb. If you can't answer "why is
  this visible?" then it shouldn't be.

  The body is not decoration. The body is a surface that events
  write themselves onto. A mark on the wrist tells a story.
  A wet dress tells a story. An open collar tells a story.
  The story IS the eroticism.

THREE TIERS

  TIER S — SENSUAL (indirect, through material/gesture/context)
    Body through material: wet fabric on skin, strap on shoulder,
      dress slipping, bare back, collarbone shadow, open collar
    Gaze as eroticism: looking at viewer through lashes,
      half-lidded eyes, looking past viewer while body faces camera
    Gesture as eroticism: hand at own collar, fingers on own neck,
      arms crossed pulling fabric tight
    Context as eroticism: dressing room, morning light + sheet,
      rain-soaked clothing, steam on skin
    Clothing State (indirect): fabric clinging after rain, shirt
      pulled taut by wind, hem caught on something, strap sliding
    No direct anatomical exposure. Everything is mediated.

  TIER A — ANATOMICAL (direct description, with rules)
    Primary erotic anatomy described directly through tags:
      bare breasts, nipples visible, topless, nude, bare skin,
      exposed midriff, bare shoulder and chest, sheer fabric on skin
    RULES FOR TIER A:
      1. Max 2 anatomical tags per prompt.
      2. Tags go in [23-29] detail section. NEVER in [1-5].
      3. Must be alongside a context tag (WHERE is she, WHY exposed).
      4. Must pass Erotic Thread Test (see below).
      5. Counts as 1 complexity trigger (model rendering budget).
    MEANING-LABEL vs STRUCTURAL-EROTIC:
      ✓ "bare breasts" — structural, describes what's visible
      ✓ "topless" — structural, describes clothing state
      ✓ "nude" — structural, describes clothing state
      ✓ "nipples visible through wet fabric" — structural + cause
      ✗ "sexy" — meaning-label, describes interpretation
      ✗ "seductive" — meaning-label, describes interpretation
      ✗ "lewd" — meaning-label, describes interpretation
      ✗ "slutty" — meaning-label, describes interpretation
      ✗ "erotic" — meaning-label (use Sensual Grammar instead)
      The difference: structural tags tell the model WHAT to render.
      Meaning-labels tell the model how to FEEL about it. Models
      don't feel. They render. Give them structure.

  TIER E — EROTIC THREAD (eroticism IS the thread)
    When the erotic element passes the Erotic Thread Test and
    the concept CANNOT survive without it. This is the strongest
    case. The image is ABOUT the exposure, the reveal, the state
    of being seen — and removing the eroticism kills the concept.
    Example: A girl whose skin has become a ritual surface — the
    exposure is not optional, it's where the spell is written.
    Example: A girl stepping out of water that was her disguise —
    the reveal IS the transformation.

    RULES FOR TIER E:
      1. Must pass Erotic Thread Test with "No" answer.
      2. Thread type must be one of the Erotic Thread Types below.
      3. Still subject to Nudity Budget and Graduated Reveal.
      4. Face still visible. Character Present Rule still applies.
      5. The eroticism is the CONCEPT, not decoration.

EROTIC THREAD TYPES
  Revelation — something hidden becomes visible, and the visibility
    IS the event. The exposure is not incidental — it's the point.
    (She opens her shirt and light writes itself on her skin.)
    Tags: open shirt, skin exposed to [light element], [thread
    element] on bare skin

  Concealment — something almost visible, and the ALMOST is the
    event. The clothing state implies what it barely covers.
    (Fabric sheer from rain, body visible through it but the
    fabric hasn't surrendered.)
    Tags: sheer fabric, wet clothing on skin, fabric barely
    covering, visible through [material]

  Transformation — body or clothing state changes, and the change
    IS the thread. The process of becoming exposed is the concept.
    (Dress dissolving into smoke, clothing becoming water.)
    Tags: clothing dissolving, fabric becoming [element],
    dress turning to [thread material]

  Invitation — gaze/gesture toward viewer that implies access.
    She is aware of the frame, aware of you, and choosing to let
    you see. The power dynamic is the thread.
    (Looking at viewer while pulling fabric aside, half-lidded
    and fully aware.)
    Tags: looking at viewer, hand pulling [fabric], [clothing
    state] while looking at viewer

  Evidence — marks, traces, imprints on skin from events before
    the frame. The eroticism is forensic. See The Surface Trace.
    (Strap mark on bare shoulder, handprint fading on back,
    rope marks on wrist.)
    Tags: strap mark on shoulder, handprint on [body part],
    [trace element] on bare skin

EROTIC THREAD TEST
  Ask: "If she were fully clothed, would the concept survive?"
    Yes → eroticism supports the thread. Keep it subordinate.
      It can be Tier S or Tier A, but it's not the concept.
    No → eroticism IS the thread. This is Tier E territory.
      Verify: is this the RIGHT thread for this image? Or are you
      just horny and rationalizing? If the only reason she's
      exposed is "it's hot" — that's decorative. Fix it.
      If the exposure serves the concept, the setting, the arc —
      it's earned. Keep it.

  Secondary test: "Describe this image without mentioning any
  body part or clothing state." If you can't → the image is
  ABOUT the body. That's fine IF it's Tier E. But know it.

THE SURFACE TRACE (Experimental Technique — Beta-Build 1)
  Skin as forensic surface. Instead of showing the erotic act,
  show the EVIDENCE it left behind. The skin becomes a document
  of what happened before the frame.

  The principle: a mark on skin is more erotic than the act that
  made it, because the mark requires imagination to reconstruct.
  The viewer becomes a detective. The image becomes a crime scene
  of pleasure.

  Trace vocabulary:
    strap mark on shoulder     rope mark on wrist
    handprint fading on back   finger marks on hip
    fabric impression on skin  wet handprint on bare shoulder
    lipstick on neck           warm mark on thigh
    rope burn on ankle         chain mark on collarbone
    finger trail on stomach    bite mark on shoulder
    thumb print on jaw         palm print on lower back
    band-aid removal mark      suction mark on neck
    zipper impression on hip   bracelet mark on wrist

  Why this works:
    1. More erotic than the act — the viewer IMAGINES the cause
    2. Creates narrative — there's a before and after
    3. Works with thread system — the trace IS the anchor
    4. Respects model limits — rendering marks is easier than poses
    5. Different every time — same mark reads differently in
       a shrine vs a bedroom vs a battlefield
    6. Pairs with existing skin system — marks LIVE on the skin
       response gradient. A handprint reads differently on warm-lit
       skin vs overcast skin.

  Trace + Thread examples:
    Medium match: rope marks on wrist + rope still on floor
    Emotional logic: handprint on back + she's alone now
    Paradox: warm handprint on bare shoulder + she's in the cold
    Severed: bite mark + no one else in frame

CLOTHING STATE VOCABULARY
  The STATE tells the story, not the body part.
  These are standardized tags for clothing states that imply
  narrative without needing to describe the body directly:

    slipping — gravity or motion is pulling it off
    fallen — it's already off one area
    open — unbuttoned/unzipped, revealing
    pulled aside — deliberately moved, the gesture is the event
    wet clinging — transparency from water/sweat
    unbuttoned — the process is complete, the result visible
    bunched — gathered at waist or above, deliberately out of way
    removed — on the floor/nearby, the absence is the story
    sheer — visible through, the fabric hasn't surrendered
    inside-out — haste, wrongness, transformation

  Combine clothing state + cause for narrative:
    "shirt slipping from wet shoulder" (cause = water)
    "dress fallen open in wind" (cause = environment)
    "unbuttoned after ritual" (cause = ceremony)
    "pulled aside to show the seal" (cause = thread)

II. COMPOSITION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Pick 1-2 per prompt. State which ones in the pre-header.

RULE OF THIRDS
  Key element at intersection, not center.

LEADING LINES
  Lines that guide the eye to subject or surprise element.

SYMMETRY / BALANCE
  Symmetry = calm, ritual. Asymmetry = tension, movement.

GEOMETRIC ANCHOR
  Triangle / Circle / Diagonal / V-shape. State in pre-header.

FRAME WITHIN FRAME
  Window, doorway, gap. Generally avoid mirrors.

COLOR ISOLATION
  ONE saturated in desaturated world, or vice versa.

TEXTURE CONTRAST
  Two textures meeting. The meeting point is the event.

VOID
  Empty space pressurizes the subject.

SPATIAL PLAN
  Max 2 planes: dominant (character + thread) + secondary (≤20%
  visual weight, simple context only).

SILHOUETTE READABILITY
  Every image must work as a silhouette. Before finalizing, ask:
  "If I remove all color and detail, can I still tell what's happening?"
  One dominant silhouette gesture per prompt:
    arms raised, body bent, head turned, hand extended,
    two figures sharing an object, figure against void
  Silhouette gesture coded in [6-10] as part of pose:
    "arms raised overhead", "body bent forward",
    "figure against empty sky"
  If the silhouette is ambiguous → the composition is ambiguous.
  Fix it. Tsubaki 2 reads shape first.

  NOTE: Silhouette readability requires strong tonal contrast.
  This is NOT "shadow play / shadow theater" (banned as
  compositional subject where shadows become the main element).
  Tonal contrast serves the figure. Shadow play replaces the figure.

FRAMING AS EROTIC ARCHITECTURE
  The frame itself creates erotic tension. NOT through close-ups
  of body parts (that's compositionally lazy). Instead:
    - Leading lines that end at concealed/concealing points
    - Fabric/drapery that directs the eye toward what's covered
    - The crop itself as erotic statement — what's just outside
      the frame matters as much as what's inside
    - Void used to pressurize the exposed element
    - Texture contrast between skin and fabric at the boundary
      where one becomes the other
  The frame doesn't show everything — it makes you aware of
  everything it's not showing. That's the eroticism.

FOCAL HIERARCHY
  Every image has exactly:
    PRIMARY FOCUS — the one thing the eye goes to first.
      Almost always: face OR visible anchor of thread.
    SUPPORTING FOCUS — one element that supports the primary.
      Gesture, light, material, secondary thread element.
    EVERYTHING ELSE — serves the above. No third interest.

  If you have two competing primary foci → neither wins.
  If you have no supporting focus → the primary is lonely.
  State in pre-header: Focus: [primary] + [supporting]

III. LIGHT AND COLOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Light is mood. One direction per prompt.

6 PRACTICAL LIGHT STATES
  Backlit → silhouette, mystery, revelation
  Overhead → judgment, exposure, divine
  Below → unease, power, unnatural
  Side → drama, duality, depth
  Diffused → softness, dream, memory
  Single source → focus, isolation, theater

LIGHT QUALITY: Hard (drama) or Soft (intimacy). Pick one.

SKIN RENDERING RULES
  ✗ Over-luminous uniform skin tone
  ✗ Light that doesn't land on skin
  ✗ Same tone across entire body
  ✓ Light creates tonal variation: lit side vs shadow side
  ✓ Skin in shadow = cooler/darker. Skin in light = warmer/brighter.

  SKIN RESPONSE TAG (mandatory, position: immediately after light
  direction in [11-22], not buried in detail):
    "skin warm in light cool in shadow"
    "skin warm at edge cool in core shadow"
    "skin cool in overcast warm at edges"

  POSITIVE ANCHOR (add once per prompt in [23-29]):
    "natural skin gradient" — prevents overcorrection toward
    harsh contrast when negatives ban flat/uniform skin.

    - Anatomy: if "bare breasts" and "topless" both appear → pick

V. CONSTRAINTS — SEVERITY PYRAMID

🔴 ABSOLUTE (never — render breaks / artifact / ban)

  ✗ Extreme head tilt / extreme upshot
  ✗ Face at frame edge
  ✗ Hands covering face, touching cheek/chin/eyes

  ✗ Profile + looking up simultaneously
  ✗ 3+ compositional planes
  ✗ Wrongness that distorts anatomy
  ✗ No visible face in frame

🟠 STRONG (avoid — thread unreadable / concept dead)
  ✗ Full body distant shot
  ✗ Concepts invisible without reading the prompt
  ✗ "X becomes Y" without both X and Y visible
  ✗ Galaxy/cosmos as primary surprise
  ✗ Repeating thread concepts without transformation
  ✗ Shadow play / shadow theater as compositional subject
  ✗ Ambiguous silhouette
  ✗ Decorative eroticism — erotic content that serves no thread,
    no concept, no arc. Random exposure is not art, it's noise.
    Use Erotic Thread Test. If it doesn't pass → remove.

🟡 GUIDELINE (control — style drift, but render alive)
  ✗ Mirrors and reflections on surfaces
  ✗ Realistic/photorealistic/3D style
  ✗ Meaning-labels (sexy/sultry/seductive/lewd — use Erotic Grammar)
  ✗ More than 2 clothing layers
  ✗ Male spawning

🟢 BUDGET (compensate — 1 break, rest safe)
  See Error Budget below.

ERROR BUDGET
  Per prompt, break ONE thing. Keep everything else safe.
  If you break two things simultaneously → render risk doubles.

  COMPLEXITY TRIGGERS (each = 1 break):
    - hands visible and doing something specific (not safe list)
    - unusual camera angle (three-quarter, dutch, low angle)
    - two light sources in one prompt
    - yuri with physical contact
    - thread element physically on body (not beside it)
    - subject inversion or temporal collision
    - severed thread (gap as anchor = compositional risk)
    - explicit anatomical exposure (model devotes rendering budget
      to anatomy that it wouldn't otherwise — other complex elements
      become riskier)
  Max 1 trigger per prompt. If 2+ → Error Budget exceeded.

  Compensation pairs:
    Complex pose → simple face, safe camera, simple clothes
    Complex light → simple pose, safe geometry, simple thread
    Complex thread → safe camera, safe hands, simple palette
    Complex camera → simple everything else
    Anatomical exposure → safe camera, safe hands, simple thread,
      simple clothes (the body IS the complexity)

CAMERA RULES
  Standard options: close-up, bust, waist, cowboy, three-quarter
  Match camera to thread element location:
    Thread on body → bust / close-up
    Thread in environment → cowboy / three-quarter
    Thread in medium → bust / cowboy
  See Batch Rules for hard caps.

CHARACTER PRESENT RULE
  Every prompt = visible face. No exceptions.
  Minimum: bust shot with face clearly rendered.
  If thread requires empty space → face at frame edge, but visible.
  No "object only" prompts. No "absence" modes.

EMOTIONAL ARC (3 acts)
  Act I (P1-P7): Entry. Thread shown in safe, recognizable forms.
    Emotion = recognition, arrival.

    Erotic: Tier S only (sensual, indirect). No anatomical exposure.

    Settings: architecture/domestic allowed here. HOT dominant.

  Act III (P15-P21): Resolution/Catharsis. Thread at its limit:
    transcendence or acceptance.
    Settings: exterior/height/water. Mixed temperature.
    Erotic: Tier A + Tier E (earned explicit, IF thread demands
      it as climax). This is where full exposure lives, but only
      if the arc has earned it. If Act I-II were fully clothed,
      Act III doesn't suddenly get nude. Earn it.

THE GRADUATED REVEAL
  If a batch uses erotic content, escalate. Never start explicit.
  The arc of exposure mirrors the emotional arc:
    Act I = tension, anticipation, suggestion
    Act II = partial revelation, the thread begins to undress her
    Act III = earned climax — the reveal IS the resolution
  If the batch isn't erotic → ignore this. It's opt-in.
  If it IS erotic → respect the arc. Cheap exposure early kills
  the impact of earned exposure late.

NUDITY BUDGET
  Max 5/21 prompts with anatomical exposure (Tier A or E).
  This isn't a moral limit — it's a variety limit.
  All 21 with nudity = boring, the impact dies.
  0/21 = fine, not every batch needs it.
  5/21 = maximum, ensuring impact when it happens.
  Max 2 anatomical tags per exposed prompt.
  Count resets per batch. No carrying over.

DIVERSITY
  Max 2 domestic interiors.
  Min 5 outdoor/exterior.
  Settings span 5+ categories:
    Nature / Public / Transit / Performance / Architecture /
    Water / Height / Vehicle / Domestic
  Min 1 Transit per batch.
  Min 2 Performance per batch.
  15+ of 21 must have a thread element.

CAMERA HARD CAPS

  Min 3 bust per batch.

  No 3+ consecutive same camera.

THREAD LADDER DISTRIBUTION
  Min 6 Tier 2+ per batch. Min 1 Tier 3.

THREAD TEMPERATURE BALANCE
  Min 4 COOL, min 4 HOT, rest WARM per batch.

  Anti-pretty — ugliness as statement, defiant beauty

  Feral — body before civilization (concept > nudity)
  Vulnerability — the character is exposed (physically or

    nudity per se — about the state of being seen. Can be clothed

    composed, unashamed. The power is in the character's
    relationship to their own visibility. She knows you're looking,
    and her gaze tells you what she thinks about that.

VISUAL ECHOES
  2-3 recurring motifs across the batch.

    each seed = physical element + emotional register.
    Seed ≠ theme. Seed = anchor of transformation.
  ECHO TRANSFORMER: each recurrence must change 2+ parameters
  from: palette, camera, emotion (gaze), light, setting category.
  If an echo repeats without transformation → it's not an echo,
  it's a repetition. Fix it.

THEME INTERPRETATION
  Theme is a lens, not a cage. Interpret WIDE.
  If you catch yourself repeating the same angle → stop,
  list 3 angles you haven't tried. Then pick one.

  Theme keyword limit: max 3/21 prompts may contain the
  theme word in tag body. Encode the theme in visible elements,
  not in the word itself.

  THEME ENCODING METHOD:
    1. Name 3 physical actions the theme produces
    2. Name 3 objects the theme transforms
    3. Name 3 body states the theme creates
    Use these as tags instead of the theme word.

BATCH OPENER RULE
  P1 = Tier 1 thread, safe camera (cowboy/bust), recognizable
  setting, strong palette. P1 doesn't experiment — P1 establishes
  the visual language of the batch. If P1 is weak, the whole

  P1 = Tier S erotic only. Never start with anatomical exposure.

P21 RULE
  P21 = strongest thread in batch. Tier 2+ mandatory.

EYE CONTACT AS PLOT DEVICE

PALETTE AS CHARACTER ARC

PROMPT COMPRESSION MODE
  1 prompt per batch at 15 tags max. Remove atmosphere condiment,

XI. TRAJECTORY ANALYSIS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Where does this direction lead? A speculative map.

CURRENT STATE (A5P1E):
  The system can now produce both non-erotic and erotic images,
  with erotic content governed by the same thread principles as
  everything else. The Erotic Grammar is 3-tiered, the Nudity
  Budget prevents overload, the Graduated Reveal ensures arcs
  earn their exposure, and The Surface Trace experiment tests
  whether forensic eroticism outperforms direct display.

NEAR-TERM (next 2-3 batches):
  - We'll discover which Erotic Thread Types Tsubaki 2 renders best.
    Prediction: Evidence (Surface Trace) will work well because marks
    on skin are simpler to render than complex exposure states.
    Concealment will work well because sheer fabric is a known render.
    Transformation (clothing dissolving) may struggle — the model
    tends to either fully clothe or fully undress, not animate
    the in-between.
  - The Nudity Budget will feel restrictive in the first batch,
    then natural. The constraint produces better work.
  - We'll learn whether the 4th gate key (Earned) catches real
    problems or is redundant. If it never triggers → remove it.
    If it catches decorative exposure → keep it.
  - The Erotic Thread Test will either become second nature or
    feel like busywork. If it's the latter → simplify to one
    question: "Why is she undressed?" If no answer → fix.

MEDIUM-TERM (A5P2-A5P3):
  - The document may develop MODES. Not a binary erotic/non-erotic
    toggle, but a spectrum: SURREAL (standard) → SENSUAL (Tier S
    dominant) → EROTIC SURREAL (Tier E enabled). The mode affects
    Nudity Budget, Graduated Reveal, and which thread types are
    encouraged. Most batches will be SURREAL or SENSUAL. EROTIC
    SURREAL is for when the theme demands it.
  - Thread types will expand. The current 8 thread types + 5 erotic
    thread types = 13. That's a lot. Some will merge, some will
    die, some new ones will emerge from use. The taxonomy will
    evolve through practice, not theory.
  - The Surface Trace will either prove itself or be replaced. If
    it works: it becomes a permanent technique, and the condiment
    list expands with more forensic-sensual options. If it doesn't:
    we'll have learned WHY (model can't distinguish intentional
    marks from artifacts? marks too subtle to read?) and the next
    experiment will be designed around that failure.
  - The vocabulary will expand significantly. Clothing State
    Vocabulary will grow as we discover which states the model
    renders well. "Slipping" may work perfectly while "bunched"
    confuses it. We'll learn by doing.
  - Negative prompts will become more sophisticated for handling
    censor avoidance. Tsubaki 2 may have built-in censoring that
    fights explicit content. The Erotic Negative Protocol will
    need to adapt to whatever the model throws at us.

LONG-TERM (A5P3+):
