§1. TONAL PACING (TP)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A batch is not 21 prompts at the same intensity. Pacing creates
memorability through contrast.

TP1 = SLOW OPEN
    P01-P03 are atmospheric, soft, low-tension. Sets mood.
    Allows P04+ to hit harder by contrast.

TP2 = RISING HOOK
    P04-P06 escalate tension. First peak at P06 (midpoint of act I).

TP3 = COMPRESSION BURST
    P07-P09 are short, sharp, high-density. Three punchy images.

TP4 = DEEP BREATH
    P10 is the longest, slowest, most atmospheric prompt.
    Counterpoint to compression burst.

TP5 = DESCENT RHYTHM
    P11-P13 build tension through accumulation, not intensity.

TP6 = DARK NIGHT
    P14 is the lowest, heaviest, slowest prompt. Maximum atmosphere.

TP7 = REVELATION CASCADE
    P15-P17 are bright, sharp, fast. Three reveals.

TP8 = CLIMAX STACK
    P18-P20 are the highest-tension prompts in the batch.

TP9 = ECHO CLOSE
    P21 echoes P01 in mood/structure but resolves the concept.

TP usage:
  - Each prompt's pre-header TP field declares its pacing role
  - Block A must use all 9 TP codes per batch
  - TP determines prompt length (TP3/TP7 = short, TP4/TP6 = long)
  - TP determines ETM ceiling (TP4/TP6 = low ETM, TP8 = high ETM)

§3. MOTIF LIBRARY (MOT) — cross-batch symbols
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Motifs are objects/symbols that recur across batches. They create
the feeling of a shared universe without meta-text.

MOT01 = THE FOLDED LETTER
    A folded letter appears in some prompts. Never opened on-camera.
    Implies connection between scenes.

MOT02 = THE BRASS KEY
    A specific brass key appears in multiple batches. Different
    contexts (on cord, in hand, on table, in lock).

MOT03 = THE WHITE FEATHER
    A single white feather drifts in some scenes. Different
    meanings (loss, freedom, omen).

MOT04 = THE CHIPPED CUP
    A specific chipped ceramic cup appears in domestic scenes.
    Implies same household across batches.

MOT05 = THE DROPPED SHOE
    One shoe lying on floor/ground. Implies hasty departure.

MOT06 = THE DEAD FLOWER
    A specific dried flower in a vase. Same flower type across
    batches, different rooms.

MOT07 = THE OPEN BOOK FACE-DOWN
    A book left open face-down. Implies interrupted reading.

MOT08 = THE UNSENT LETTER
    Envelope with broken wax seal, never sent.

MOT09 = THE BURNED MEAL
    A pot on stove with burned food. Same pot across batches.

MOT10 = THE CRACKED MIRROR
    A mirror with single crack. Different rooms, same crack pattern.

MOT usage:
  - 2-3 MOT items appear per batch
  - MOT appears in prompt body as environmental detail, not focal
  - Never named as "motif" — just present
  - Cross-batch recognition is the point

§5. MEMORABILITY SCORE (MEM)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Every prompt gets subjective MEM score 1-5 at design time.

MEM1 = forgettable. Generic. Could be any character.
MEM2 = slightly memorable. One detail sticks.
MEM3 = memorable. Multiple details stick.
MEM4 = highly memorable. Strong visual identity.
MEM5 = iconic. Cannot be mistaken for any other character.

MEM criteria:
  - VIS signature present and specific?
  - AXI archetype (if VOLT) or PAN pantheon (if NICHE) used?
  - Concept (CL/CTP) makes image memorable beyond body?
  - Composition (CW) is non-default?
  - Motif (MOT) present?
  - Setting unusual for character type?

MEM quota per batch:
  MEM1: 0 (no forgettable prompts allowed)
  MEM2: max 4
  MEM3: min 8
  MEM4: min 6
  MEM5: min 3 (forces iconic moments)

MEM declared in pre-header MEM field.
